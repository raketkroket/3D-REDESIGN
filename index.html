NEBULA Xplorer Satellite

NEBULA Xplorer is a cutting-edge X-ray satellite exploring extreme objects like black holes and neutron stars. By tracking how X-ray emissions change over time, it reveals what is happening in these intense environments.

This student-driven mission combines innovation with academic and industry expertise. Its high time resolution and continuous observations reveal how matter behaves, how jets form, and how these systems evolve.

X-Ray Instrument

Sending a telescope to space means engineering for the impossible: it has to survive a rocket launch, then operate flawlessly in a vacuum where temperatures swing by hundreds of degrees. NEBULA-Xplorer's X-ray instrument is suspended on three precision titanium mounts that isolate it from shock and temperature change, anchored to a reinforced panel, and oriented to use every centimeter of space inside the rocket fairing.

Star Tracker Module

To observe a faint X-ray source hundreds of light-years away, NEBULA-Xplorer needs to hold its aim with extraordinary precision. Star trackers continuously scan the sky, recognize patterns of stars, and determine the spacecraft's orientation down to a few arcseconds. Two units ensure constant visibility of stars even during rotation.

Dawn 4U CubeDrive

The propulsion system counters atmospheric drag, performs collision avoidance, and enables controlled reentry. This compact thruster unit carries under two kilograms of propellant and is precisely aligned to ensure stable, accurate maneuvers throughout the mission.

S-Band Antenna

The S-band antenna enables communication with Earth during brief contact windows. It transmits gigabytes of scientific data over large distances in minutes, ensuring valuable observations reach ground stations.

Sun Sensor

Sun sensors provide basic orientation by detecting the Sun's position. They are crucial during emergencies or initial deployment, helping the spacecraft maintain power and avoid dangerous pointing scenarios.

Magnetorquers

These external devices generate magnetic torque to control the satellite's orientation and desaturate reaction wheels. By interacting with Earth's magnetic field, they provide efficient three-axis attitude control without the complexity of thrusters.

Solar Panel

These panels convert sunlight into electricity to power the satellite's systems and instruments. Positioned to capture maximum sunlight, they support continuous operation when combined with onboard energy storage during eclipse.
